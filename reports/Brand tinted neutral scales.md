# Bend Bland's grays toward any brand

The best way for Bland to tint its Neutral scale toward each project's brand is to **reuse Bland's own neutral chroma curve as a fixed shape, put it at the brand's hue, and scale its height by a capped brand-chroma factor and a per-hue factor, all inside the `npm run brand` generator, which then writes 13 static `--neutral-*` hex steps into the override file next to the 13 `--brand-*` steps**. No shipping system does this at runtime: Radix, Tailwind, shadcn and Park UI pick a pre-made gray family per accent, and Material computes neutrals in JavaScript at the source hue with a small fixed chroma. Bland's neutrals are already strong for a "neutral" (peak OKLCH C 0.033, about HCT chroma 16, the level Material uses for a secondary *accent*), so copying that amplitude onto yellow, orange, red or green would read about 1.6 times more tinted than it does on blue; the per-hue factor exists to stop that. Lightness stays fixed per step, so contrast barely moves: in a test across eleven brands, step 600 on white stayed within 6.10 to 6.24:1 against Bland's 6.16:1. CSS relative color is well supported in 2026, but it would cost Bland its offline contrast check and its in-Node gamut mapping, so it should not be the shipped contract. Before anything is built, Godwin needs to settle six things: whether tinting is on by default, which strength presets exist, whether yellow and red brands rotate hue, how near-black brands behave, whether white, black and the alpha neutrals stay pure, and what Figma shows for a client brand.

## Bland's hand-tuned blue gray is already a "colored gray"

Bland's Figma Neutral primitives, converted to OKLCH (my computation from the raw export in `tokens/figma/raw/01.json`), give a clear bell. Steps 0 and 1000 are pure white and black. Chroma rises from **0.0017 at step 50 (L 0.961)** through 0.0075 (100), 0.0154 (200) and 0.0220 (300) to **0.0309 at 400 and 0.0334 at 600 (L 0.494)**, with a small hand-tuned dip to 0.0292 at 500. It then falls to 0.0279 (700), 0.0199 (800), 0.0110 (900) and 0.0092 (950). The hue holds at 261 throughout, the same as `--brand-600` (`#0062ff`, OKLCH 0.556 / 0.245 / 261). Measured against the sRGB gamut at hue 261, every step uses **about 12 to 20 percent of the available chroma**. That is why the curve tapers at both ends: it is effectively a constant share of a gamut that narrows toward white and black ([Ottosson, Oklab](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/), computed).

The field puts that amplitude in context. Material's default Tonal Spot neutral is HCT chroma 5 to 6, which for a blue source converts to about **OKLCH 0.010**, roughly a third of Bland's peak, and its secondary palette sits at HCT 16 ([material-color-utilities dynamic_scheme.ts](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/typescript/dynamiccolor/dynamic_scheme.ts), [core_palette.ts](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/typescript/palettes/core_palette.ts)). Radix's hand-made grays peak at 0.010 (sage, olive, sand) to 0.019 (mauve) ([@radix-ui/colors](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@radix-ui/colors)). GitHub Primer peaks around 0.023 ([@primer/primitives](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@primer/primitives)) and IBM Carbon's coolGray around 0.015 ([@carbon/colors](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@carbon/colors)). Tailwind v4 spans the range: zinc peaks at 0.017, gray at 0.034 and slate at **0.046**, and the newer mauve, olive, mist and taupe neutrals peak at 0.021 to 0.034 ([Tailwind theme.css](https://github.com/tailwindlabs/tailwindcss/blob/main/packages/tailwindcss/theme.css)). Bland sits beside Tailwind gray, olive and mauve, clearly in "colored gray" territory. That is a legitimate taste, but it leaves the generator little headroom: whatever works for blue at 0.033 is near the ceiling for other hues.

The shape is sound. Tailwind's v4.2 neutrals follow the same bell (about 0.002 to 0.005 at 50/100, peak at 500, falling to 0.004 to 0.008 at 950), and Preline's published generator guidance prescribes a bell peaking at 0.020 at step 500, with the explicit rule that the dark end carries less chroma than the light end "so dark backgrounds stay clean" ([Preline palette-guidance.md](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/preline@4.2.0/skills/theme-generator/docs/palette-guidance.md)). Material's alternative, a constant chroma across all tones, comes out nearly flat in OKLCH and depends on the gamut to save it near white and black ([dynamic_scheme.ts](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/typescript/dynamiccolor/dynamic_scheme.ts)). Open Props, which uses one constant chroma for every step, has an author who warns that its grays look "funky" under 10 percent lightness ([nerdy.dev](https://nerdy.dev/open-props-oklch-palettes-beta)). No system examined changes the curve's *shape* by hue; they change only amplitude and hue. Radix makes this split explicit: the shape comes from a reference gray, the amplitude from the input color, and the hue is held constant ([generate-radix-colors.tsx](https://github.com/radix-ui/website/blob/main/components/generate-radix-colors.tsx)). Bland should do the same, with its own curve as the reference.

## Equal OKLCH chroma is not equal tint, and yellow proves it

OKLCH is the right working space. It predicts lightness and chroma far better than CIELAB (RMS error L 0.20 versus 1.70), and unlike CIELCh it keeps blue blue as chroma changes ([Ottosson](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/), [Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/oklch-in-css-why-quit-rgb-hsl), [Levien](https://raphlinus.github.io/color/2021/01/18/oklab-critique.html)). OKLCH chroma is not perceptually equal across hues at these low levels, though. Material's HCT chroma 8 converts to about **OKLCH 0.015 for blue but 0.021 to 0.027 for yellow and 0.020 to 0.026 for red**, so the same OKLCH number reads about 1.6 times more tinted on warm hues than on blue (computed with `@material/material-color-utilities` from [dynamic_scheme.ts](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/typescript/dynamiccolor/dynamic_scheme.ts)). Vision science points the same way. Shifts toward blue are more readily accepted as achromatic than equal shifts toward yellow ([Basim et al. 2025](https://goddardlab.psy.unsw.edu.au/assets/files/basim2025.pdf)), and saturation is perceived unequally across hue quadrants ([Hedjar, Toscani, Gegenfurtner 2025](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/40927/1/josaa-42-5-b305.pdf)). This is why most tinted UI grays are blue. A yellow-green tint at a given chroma reads as aged paper or khaki, while a blue tint at the same chroma reads as cool and clean.

Shipped palettes encode the correction by hand. Tailwind's warm stone peaks at 0.013, about 0.3 times slate's 0.046, and taupe at 0.021, while olive (hue 107) reaches 0.031 ([Tailwind theme.css](https://github.com/tailwindlabs/tailwindcss/blob/main/packages/tailwindcss/theme.css)). Material flags yellow neutrals (HCT hue 105 to 125) and, in its Expressive dark scheme, drops their chroma from 14 to 6 ([hct.ts](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/typescript/hct/hct.ts), [dynamic_scheme.ts](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/typescript/dynamiccolor/dynamic_scheme.ts)). Radix pairs warm accents with *rotated* grays rather than same-hue ones: tomato (hue 33) gets mauve (about hue 300), amber (84) gets sand (90 to 107), and green (158) gets sage (165 to 174) ([color-gray-pairs.tsx](https://github.com/radix-ui/website/blob/main/components/color-gray-pairs.tsx), converted from [@radix-ui/colors](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@radix-ui/colors)). Tailwind's warm neutrals drift from yellowish lights toward orange-red darks (stone from hue 106 to 49 to 73), which keeps dark yellows from turning olive ([Tailwind theme.css](https://github.com/tailwindlabs/tailwindcss/blob/main/packages/tailwindcss/theme.css)). Programmatic systems, by contrast, hold the exact source hue (Material, and the Radix generator's `H = source.H`).

The practical answer for Bland is a **per-hue multiplier on the peak, with the exact brand hue as the default**. Relative to blue (1.0), the multiplier is roughly 0.95 to 1.0 for hues 230 to 300 (blue to violet), about 0.85 for magenta, 0.65 to 0.70 for reds, and 0.45 to 0.55 for orange, yellow and green (60 to 160), recovering toward 0.75 for cyan. These numbers are fitted to Material's HCT conversion and to the gap between Tailwind's warm and cool neutrals, not to a perception study; none of the sources publishes a test of where "tinted" turns into "dirty." The Helmholtz-Kohlrausch brightness boost is negligible at chroma under 0.035 and needs no correction ([Wikipedia, H-K effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmholtz%E2%80%93Kohlrausch_effect), [High, Green, Nussbaum 2023](https://hdl.handle.net/11250/3110488)). A gamut-relative rule ("15 percent of the sRGB maximum at this L and hue") sounds elegant because it reproduces Bland's blue curve, but it fails the other way: at L 0.92 the gamut allows 0.101 at hue 90 against 0.038 at hue 261, so it would hand yellow brands the most tint exactly where it reads worst (computed from [Ottosson](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/)). The gamut should act only as a ceiling.

## Brand strength, gray brands and near-black brands need a ramp, not a constant

Material's default scheme gives every source the same neutral chroma, so a neon brand and a dusty brand of the same hue get identical grays. Its Content and Fidelity variants scale with the source instead (chroma divided by 8, or `min(chroma/12, 4)` in the legacy palette), and Radix scales its reference curve by the input's chroma, capped at 1.5 times the input ([core_palette.ts](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/typescript/palettes/core_palette.ts), [generate-radix-colors.tsx](https://github.com/radix-ui/website/blob/main/components/generate-radix-colors.tsx)). For Bland, a fixed rule would tint the grays of a muted brand more than the brand itself, and a purely proportional rule would make neon brands produce tinted grays. A **ramp with a floor and a ceiling** solves both: below brand chroma 0.02 the neutrals are pure gray, because the hue of a near-achromatic color is noise and must not decide the grays; from 0.02 to 0.12 the tint ramps up linearly; above 0.12, which covers nearly every solid brand, the strength is full. Radix's own fallback for pure white or black accents (rebuilding them from the gray scale) supports the floor.

Near-black brands are the open case. A dark navy such as `#14213d` has an absolute chroma of only 0.056, yet it uses about a third of the chroma available at its lightness; `#111827` has 0.032 and uses about a fifth (my computation). On the absolute ramp, the navy gets a peak neutral chroma of 0.012 (a quiet cool gray) and the near-black gets 0.005 (nearly pure). A gamut-relative ramp (Okhsl-style saturation, [Ottosson, Okhsv and Okhsl](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/colorpicker/)) would tint both more. Neither is wrong. It depends on whether a "black" brand should feel monochrome or cool, so it belongs on Godwin's list rather than in the algorithm.

Dark mode needs no second curve. Bland mirrors its Neutral scale (0 and 950 swap, 50 and 900 swap, and so on), so dark surfaces read steps 900 to 1000, where the profile is already low (0.011 and 0.009 for blue, about 0.005 and 0.004 after the yellow multiplier). The worry about dark yellows turning olive is real for an absolute rule, where the same chroma takes 26 to 29 percent of the yellow gamut at L 0.15 to 0.21 against 12 to 14 percent for blue (computed). The hue multiplier already cuts yellow to 0.45, which handles it. Radix does keep separate light and dark reference grays ([generate-radix-colors.tsx](https://github.com/radix-ui/website/blob/main/components/generate-radix-colors.tsx)), but that comes from its different lightness model, not from a chroma need Bland shares.

Contrast is almost untouched when lightness is held. Tinting at a fixed OKLCH L shifts luminance by under 0.2 percent for blue and yellow, but by about 2 percent for red-orange and green at Bland's peak chroma, which moves WCAG ratios by roughly 0.1 at mid tones (computed with the WCAG 2 formula; APCA is also luminance-only, [APCA introduction](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-introduction)). The eleven-brand test below bears that out. Green neutrals come out slightly lighter (lower contrast on white) and reds slightly darker, so anything sitting exactly on 4.5:1 can tip. Bland's neutral step 500 is already at 4.17:1 on white, so the check must cover neutral pairings and not assume them.

## Static steps from the generator beat runtime relative color

Relative color syntax is now broadly available (full support in Chrome and Edge 131+, Safari 18.0+, Firefox 133+, about 88 percent global usage with 5 percent more partial), and `color-mix()` reaches about 95 percent ([caniuse, relative colors](https://caniuse.com/css-relative-colors), [caniuse, color-mix](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_types_color_color-mix)). So a runtime formula such as `oklch(from var(--brand-600) 0.494 calc(c * 0.136) h)` per step would work in most browsers. It is still the wrong contract for Bland, for four reasons. First, it ties tint to the brand's raw chroma with no hue factor, floor or cap unless all of that is expressed in CSS math. Second, a near-gray brand's hue resolves to `none` or noise at runtime. Third, browsers *clip* out-of-gamut `oklch()` instead of gamut-mapping it, and as of mid-2025 the CSS Working Group had not settled the algorithm ([public-css-archive, June 2025](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-css-archive/2025Jun/0124.html)). Fourth, and decisively, the brand tool's contrast check reads the override file in Node; with runtime color it could only verify contrast by rendering pages in Playwright. There are other traps: `@supports` with a `var()` inside always passes, and an invalid relative color in a custom property falls back to `unset`, not to an earlier declaration ([MDN, relative colors](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/CSS_colors/Relative_colors)). Every precedent found ships neutrals as static values chosen at build or init time: Radix's auto-pairing, Park UI, Tailwind, shadcn's `baseColor` at `init`, daisyUI themes, Material's generator ([Radix Themes](https://www.radix-ui.com/themes/docs/theme/color), [Park UI](https://park-ui.com/docs/theme/colors), [shadcn theming](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/theming), [daisyUI](https://daisyui.com/docs/themes/)). The only live knob found is Open Props' beta `--gray-hue` and `--gray-chroma` ([nerdy.dev](https://nerdy.dev/open-props-oklch-palettes-beta)).

Bland's brand override already sets the pattern. In `docs/brand.md`, every Theme token whose alias chain ends in a brand step becomes `var(--brand-N)` (57 tokens), and the generator's `fromOklch` already reduces chroma until a color fits sRGB. Neutrals should follow the same path. `build-tokens.ts` emits the 13 `--neutral-*` steps at `:root` with Figma's values, and switches every Theme token that resolves to a Neutral primitive to `var(--neutral-N)` (the notes estimate 82 tokens), with dark mode still pointing at the mirrored steps. The lint keeps components off `--neutral-*` as it does for `--brand-*`. The generator then writes 26 values instead of 13. A strength dial, the one ergonomic advantage of a runtime approach, becomes a *generator input* (`--neutral none|subtle|full`, plus a slider on the docs-site Brand theme page), not runtime CSS. There is one parity catch: running the algorithm on Bland's own blue reproduces every step except 500 (`#737d8e` against Figma's `#727d8e`, a rounding difference), so the default file must carry Figma's exact values, and only client overrides are generated.

Alpha neutrals should follow the solids the way brand alphas already do. Bland turns its three brand alpha steps into `color-mix(in srgb, var(--brand-600) 12%, transparent)` (docs/brand.md), and Tailwind's `--alpha()` helper does the same on variable colors ([Tailwind colors](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/colors)). Radix ships solved per-background alpha twins for every step ([Radix Themes](https://www.radix-ui.com/themes/docs/theme/color)), but Bland's four neutral alphas (a6, a10, a24, a40) are plain opacities of one source, so `color-mix` from that source's `--neutral-*` step keeps today's meaning. At those opacities the tint is barely visible. Tinting them is about consistency (no blue cast in a red-brand modal backdrop or shadow), and leaving them pure is an equally defensible taste.

## The recommended algorithm, with numbers

Start from Bland's Figma Neutral primitives. For each step *s*, keep its OKLCH lightness L(s) exactly, and define the profile P(s) as that step's chroma divided by the peak 0.0334. P is 0, 0.05, 0.22, 0.46, 0.66, 0.93, 0.87, 1.00, 0.84, 0.60, 0.33, 0.28, 0 for steps 0 through 1000. Steps 0 and 1000 stay pure white and black whatever the brand, which also keeps `Toggle/knob-rest` white.

Take the brand's hue *h* and chroma *Cb* from the generator's input color (the color it places on step 600). Compute the strength ramp t = clamp((Cb − 0.02) / (0.12 − 0.02), 0, 1). Compute the hue factor f(h) by linear interpolation through (0, 0.70), (30, 0.65), (60, 0.50), (90, 0.45), (120, 0.45), (160, 0.55), (200, 0.75), (230, 0.95), (260, 1.00), (290, 1.00), (320, 0.85), (360, 0.70). Multiply by a preset *k*: none 0, subtle 0.5, full 1.0. Each step's chroma is then C(s) = min(0.0334 × P(s) × t × f(h) × k, 0.20 × Cmax(L(s), h)), where Cmax is the sRGB gamut maximum, which the existing binary search in `fromOklch` already finds. The hue is *h* at every step. Convert each step to hex with `fromOklch`, write it as `--neutral-N` beside the brand steps, and run the extended check: the existing brand `PAIRS` plus every Text, Icon and Border on Surface pairing that touches a neutral, in both modes. Where a pair misses, nudge that step's L by about ±0.005, as `suggest` already does for brand steps. Pin the behaviour with generator unit tests over the eleven brands in the table above, and add a Playwright story set rendering two or three overrides in both themes.

Two variants are worth keeping behind flags, not in the default. One is a dark-end hue drift for yellow and orange brands (hue 60 to 120 rotating about 15 to 20 degrees toward orange in steps 700 to 950, as Tailwind's stone does). The other is a Radix-style rotation of red brands toward a cooler mauve-gray. Both rest on hand-tuned precedent rather than measurement.

The decisions Godwin needs to make before this is built are concrete. **Default:** is tinting on (full) for every generated brand, or opt-in, with today's blue grays used regardless of brand? **Presets:** are none, subtle and full the right dial, and should the Brand theme page expose a continuous slider? **Hue:** should neutrals always use the exact brand hue, or should yellow and red brands rotate (stone-style drift, mauve-style cooling)? **Near-black and navy brands:** should their grays stay nearly pure (absolute ramp) or turn noticeably cool (gamut-relative ramp)? **What stays pure:** are steps 0 and 1000 always white and black (recommended), and are the four alpha neutrals and the elevation shadows tinted or kept neutral? **Figma:** does a client's tinted neutral ever exist in Figma (a client copy of the file with regenerated Neutral primitives), or is it code-only, with Figma keeping Bland's blue grays as the default and the drift check comparing only the default file?

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "how do we derive a gray from a brand" to "how do we stop Bland's unusually strong gray from misbehaving on hues it was never tuned for." The bell shape, the fixed lightness and the static-generation architecture are all low-risk, and each has direct precedent. The risk sits entirely in amplitude: at 0.033, Bland's blue neutral already uses a secondary-accent level of chroma, so the per-hue factor and the brand-strength ramp do the real work, and their numbers are fitted to shipped palettes rather than measured. That makes the docs-site Brand theme page more than a preview. It is the place where Godwin's eye calibrates f(h) against real yellow, green and red client brands, and where a "subtle" preset (half today's strength, close to Primer and Tailwind olive) may turn out to be the better default for warm brands than full strength.
